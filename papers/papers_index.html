The Anthropic Incident Reveals What AI Governance Is Missing

A civilian certification body for AI, independent of both government and industry, proposed after an export control directive took two frontier models offline worldwide in a single evening.

On June 12, 2026, a US export control directive required Anthropic to suspend access to its two most capable models by any foreign national, and the company disabled them for every customer worldwide within hours. The directive cited national security authorities and provided no specific detail. This commentary argues the failure was architectural rather than individual, and proposes a civilian certification body — industry-funded, government-audited, accountable to the public — as the missing institutional layer between a contested claim and a consequential order. A September postscript records that the controls were lifted after eighteen days.

Applying a Constitutional Framework to the Middle East Conflict

Trust Architecture: a direct test.

Trust Architecture proposes that security pursued without trust does not resolve conflict but relocates and deepens it. This article tests that claim against one of the most entrenched conflicts in modern history, applying five of the framework's seven constitutional conditions and setting out a practical alternative built on verification, equal trade, and shared benefit rather than mutual threat.
